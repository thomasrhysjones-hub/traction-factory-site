An affiliate programme can become a substantial acquisition channel for an iGaming operator. It can also become a collection of expensive deals, poor tracking and arguments about player value.

The mistake is treating the launch of an affiliate platform as the launch of an affiliate business.

I built an affiliate function from scratch earlier in my career, and the most useful lesson is that **the operating model matters at least as much as the recruitment pitch**.

Here is where I'd start.

## 1. Get clear on the economics before recruiting

Before speaking to an affiliate, understand what a new customer is worth and what the business can afford to pay.

This means looking beyond the initial deposit or first-month revenue. You need a credible view of acquisition cost, retention, bonuses, payment costs, risk, and the commercial effects of revenue-share or CPA agreements over time.

There is no single payment model that is best for every partner.

**CPA** can provide a relatively clear acquisition cost, but qualification criteria and fraud controls become critical.

**Revenue share** ties partner earnings to player revenue over time, but definitions, deductions and negative-carryover rules must be unambiguous.

**Hybrid agreements** may balance upfront cost with longer-term value, while introducing more complexity to reconcile.

A model that wins affiliate sign-ups but destroys player economics isn't growth. It's an expensive reporting error.

## 2. Build measurement people can trust

Affiliate relationships deteriorate quickly when the programme cannot explain how referrals, registrations, qualified customers and commissions are measured.

Before you scale, settle the basics:

- Clear tracking and attribution rules.
- Consistent definitions for qualified acquisitions and revenue.
- Transparent reporting and payment processes.
- An escalation path for discrepancies.
- Controls for suspicious activity and duplicate or ineligible customers.

A bespoke tracking or management system can be appropriate for some businesses, but there is no need to build one simply for the sake of ownership. Reliability and trust come first.

## 3. Make compliance part of recruitment

iGaming affiliate activity operates within different rules depending on the licence, geography and marketing channel.

An operator needs to know which territories a partner targets, how it promotes the brand and whether that activity complies with applicable advertising and consumer protection requirements.

Commercial teams and compliance teams should agree the rules before recruitment scales. Approving a partner without understanding its acquisition methods can create risks well beyond the commission paid.

## 4. Recruit for relevance, not just numbers

A list of hundreds of registered affiliates can look impressive and achieve very little.

What matters is finding partners with an audience aligned to the operator's product, target markets and brand. That might include comparison sites, content publishers, communities or other legitimate traffic partners.

Partner due diligence should cover traffic sources, audience relevance, historical performance where verifiable, marketing practices and the realistic work required to activate the relationship.

Recruiting a partner is only the first step. Getting them live, correctly tracking and producing sustainable customers is what counts.

## 5. Give affiliate managers a genuine job to do

Affiliate management isn't simply answering emails and increasing commission rates.

A good manager understands a partner's acquisition methods, its operational constraints, what performance data suggests and which commercial changes are worth testing.

Account reviews should focus on customer quality, the acquisition funnel, economics, retention and practical next actions.

That also means being prepared to stop or renegotiate activity when it fails the commercial or compliance test.

## 6. Measure the programme as a business

In the early stages, I'd keep the dashboard relatively simple:

| Measure | What it tells you |
| --- | --- |
| Active, producing affiliates | Whether the programme is genuinely activated |
| Qualified new customers | Whether partnerships are delivering acquisition |
| Acquisition cost by partner | Whether growth is commercially sustainable |
| Customer value over time | Whether the channel brings valuable players |
| Payment and tracking disputes | Whether the operating model is working |
| Concentration of channel revenue | How dependent the business is on a few partners |

These measures need to be segmented by market and partner type when the programme becomes more sophisticated.

Activity is not a substitute for economics.

## Launch small. Learn quickly. Scale deliberately.

The best launch plan is rarely 'sign as many affiliates as possible'.

Build the commercial model, make tracking dependable, recruit a focused first group, learn what those relationships produce and fix the problems before scaling.

That is less glamorous than announcing a huge partner network. It is also far more likely to create a programme the business can rely on.
