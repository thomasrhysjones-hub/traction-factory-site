Where distribution breaks down.

More integrations and more logos aren't always the same as more commercially productive distribution.

01 / Route to market

Access without a clear priority

A studio may reach hundreds of operators indirectly yet lack a realistic target list. We look at market fit, aggregator coverage, licensing constraints, product positioning and where direct relationships are genuinely worth pursuing.

02 / Activation

Signed doesn't mean live

Certification, game configuration, commercial approvals and operator launch queues can all delay revenue. A useful distribution pipeline tracks each handover rather than treating the contract as the finish line.

03 / Visibility

Live doesn't mean discovered

Lobby position, portfolio relevance, release calendars and promotional support affect whether players encounter the games. A commercial plan should define what the studio and operator each need to deliver after launch.

04 / Commercial economics

Headline revenue hides the cost

Aggregator fees, promotional commitments, reporting adjustments and account servicing can change the value of a deal. We distinguish contracted access, active distribution and contribution to revenue.

What should improve?

The measures depend on the product and route to market, but they should connect commercial activity to revenue.

Distribution coverage

Target operators reached through credible direct and aggregation routes, with realistic prioritisation rather than indiscriminate breadth.

Launch conversion

Time from agreement to live status, percentage of agreed launches activated, and causes of delay.

Commercial performance

Active operator revenue, revenue per live account, position and promotion impact, and the incremental contribution after costs.

Account ownership

Specific next actions, operator development plans and a review rhythm that focuses on decisions rather than status updates.
