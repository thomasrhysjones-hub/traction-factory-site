Why Railven

A SaaS model for a SaaS problem.

Traditional aggregation often prices access as a percentage of gaming performance. Railven prices the actual platform activity instead: requests processed, data moved, environments operated and optional human services delivered.

Usage pricing

Meter the calls. Price the work.

Railven's proposed billing unit is a Railven Unit (RU). It maps technical activity to a simple invoice while still reflecting the underlying infrastructure cost. The numbers below are working commercial assumptions for validation, not final contractual tariffs.

Anything that makes Railven do production work is metered. Heavier operations consume more units.

Pricing rules

Simple enough to explain on one page.

The commercial model should stay boring. If Railven needs a spreadsheet and three sales calls to explain the bill, we have recreated the thing we are trying to replace.

Progressive tiers

Higher monthly usage automatically moves through cheaper tiers. No renegotiation required.

Minimum, not membership

A monthly minimum is credited against usage and exists only to cover the always-on production footprint.

Failures aren't revenue

Railven-originated retries and failed internal processing should not inflate a customer's bill.

Pass-through stays visible

Certification, dedicated infrastructure and third-party regulatory costs remain explicit rather than buried in a percentage.

Studio side

Make route-to-market a product.

Railven can monetise studio pain without taking a perpetual cut of every spin. Technical onboarding and distribution services should have clear scope, clear fees and a clear end point.

Studios pay for enablement. Operators pay for usage.

That separation gives Railven two revenue engines without muddling them together.

What still needs proving

This is a model to validate, not a pricing religion.

The usage principle is strong. The exact RU weights and euro rates need real traffic traces and architecture benchmarks before they become a tariff card.

Technical validation

  • Calls generated by a typical launch and game session
  • Average request / response payload sizes
  • Retry and failure rates
  • Database, cache, event-stream and logging work per endpoint
  • Peak-to-average traffic and required multi-region redundancy

Commercial validation

  • Operator willingness to buy on usage rather than GGR
  • Expected monthly RU volumes by operator tier
  • Minimum acceptable SaaS gross margin
  • Value of premium SLA / dedicated infrastructure
  • Studio willingness to pay fixed onboarding and market-service fees
