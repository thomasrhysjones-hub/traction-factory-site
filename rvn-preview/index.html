01 / The premise

The games aren't the problem. The cost of connecting them is.

Traditional aggregation often adds a percentage-based fee between operators and game studios. That fee grows with gaming revenue, even when the underlying job is technical distribution.

Railven is being developed as an alternative: a metered infrastructure service that lets both sides retain their commercial agreements. No forced change to the way content is licensed.

06 / Straight answers

The details
matter.

Does Railven replace studio royalties?

No. Operators and studios keep their existing content agreements. Railven's infrastructure invoice is separate from those payments, and any distribution requires the appropriate permissions.

Does Railven take a percentage of GGR?

The proposed core aggregation service does not. It charges for completed game rounds and successful game launches. The calculator uses GGR only to compare costs.

Do wins and losses create different charges?

No. The intended unit is a completed logical game round, with the same fee regardless of outcome. Supporting balance, settlement and retry messages are included within a defined normal-use envelope.

Will every operator save money?

Not necessarily. Savings depend on round volumes, GGR per round, the existing aggregation fee and contractual scope. Use the calculator as an illustration, not a quote.

Is the platform live?

Not yet. Railven is a pre-launch concept seeking design partners. Infrastructure, integrations, rights, security, regulatory position and final pricing remain subject to development and validation.

Do studios have to subscribe to get distributed?

No paid studio subscription is proposed as a condition for eligibility. Optional subscriptions would pay for software functionality, not operator access or commercial approvals.
