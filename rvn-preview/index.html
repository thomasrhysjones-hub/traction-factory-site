How the model works

One set of rails. Clear commercial boundaries.

Operators should see what they pay for technology. Studios should see what they earn for content. Neither needs another opaque layer between them.

Operator

Selects eligible games and connects its wallet and session systems to the published API.

→
Railven

Routes approved traffic, attributes each logical round, records events and issues an infrastructure usage invoice.

→
Studio

Operates its game server and receives content remuneration directly under the operator–studio agreement.

THE RAILVEN BILL
Technical usage only

Railven charges operators for eligible rounds and launches. It does not take a percentage of GGR.

STUDIO CONTENT RIGHTS
Existing agreements continue

Operators still pay each studio according to their own negotiated contract. Those payments are not Railven revenue.

Questions worth asking

Transparency includes the small print.

Does Railven replace game studio royalties?

No. Operators continue paying their game studios under existing licence agreements. Railven separately invoices the infrastructure it delivers.

Is Railven charging a percentage of GGR?

The proposed infrastructure tariff is based on completed rounds and game launches, not gaming revenue. GGR appears in the cost explorer only to compare your current arrangements.

Does a win generate a separate fee?

No. The intended meter is one uniquely identified completed logical round regardless of win or loss. Technical retries, settlements and idempotent duplicates would be governed by explicit fair-use and metering rules.

Can you guarantee savings?

No. Savings depend on your existing aggregator fee, actual activity, stake profile, provider contracts and jurisdiction. The calculator lets you change those assumptions.

Can we go live immediately?

Not yet. Railven is being developed. Production access will depend on validated systems, rights from providers and completion of applicable regulatory and security checks.

Are studios required to pay for distribution?

The proposed network separates optional SaaS services from eligibility to participate in distribution. No studio should be led to believe a software subscription guarantees operator access.
