The problem

Aggregation became a toll road.

Many aggregators still monetise by taking a percentage of gaming revenue, despite much of the underlying work being integration, hosting, support, certification, data delivery and account management. That misaligns price with cost, penalises successful operators and makes commercial onboarding slower than the technical work needs to be.

Capital-light route

Prove the economics before building the empire.

With no starting capital, the investable case should begin with validation and design partners, then fund a narrow MVP that proves integration speed, cost-to-serve and willingness to pay. Building a giant catalogue before proving demand would be the expensive way round.

FAQ

The obvious questions.

The model is deliberately simple. The hard part is execution: regulatory coverage, game certification, integrations, support reliability and enough catalogue depth to matter.

Is this really “no rev share”?

That is the design principle. Revenue comes from infrastructure usage and explicit services. Studio commercial agreements may still require separate economics depending on content ownership and distribution rights.

Can integration really be instant?

Not in every case. The goal is to eliminate avoidable waiting through standard APIs, sandboxes, automated credentials and self-service configuration. Certification and regulated-market requirements can still create real lead time.

Why would operators switch?

Lower variable cost at scale, fewer commercial negotiations, clearer billing and a faster operating model. The proposition only works if catalogue depth and technical reliability are credible.

Why would studios pay?

Because distribution is valuable. The studio offer must solve concrete problems: integration readiness, certification, operator access, launch execution and ongoing catalogue operations.

What becomes the moat?

Standardisation, automation, operator distribution, studio supply, regulated-market coverage, reliability and data. The pricing model gets attention. The network and operational quality create defensibility.

What should be built first?

Not a huge platform. Start with a narrow technical layer and manual operational support behind it. Prove cost-to-serve, partner demand and a repeatable integration before expanding.
