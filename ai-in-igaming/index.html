AI in iGaming / A practical perspective

Beyond the hype.
Into the work.

AI can improve how gaming businesses sell, operate and make decisions. It can also make a bad process considerably faster.

The question isn't whether to use AI. It's where it creates value, what it needs to work and when a human should stay in charge.

Explore the opportunities

Start with the distinction

AI isn't a single product. A rules-based automation, a predictive model and a generative AI assistant solve different problems. Treating them as interchangeable is an expensive way to find that out.

01 / Automation

Remove repetitive work

Move data between systems, route requests, flag missing fields, assemble reports and follow defined workflows. Often the fastest commercial return doesn't require a large language model at all.

02 / Predictive analysis

Spot patterns worth acting on

Forecast account performance, detect anomalies and identify potential opportunities. Useful when the data is consistent, the model is tested and someone understands what a false signal costs.

03 / Generative AI

Turn information into usable work

Prepare briefs, summarise contracts, structure account plans and explain performance changes. It can save time, but fluent language is not proof that an answer is correct.

04 / Human judgement

Own the decision

Commercial negotiations, sensitive customer issues, regulatory obligations and strategic trade-offs need accountable people. AI should make those decisions better informed, not obscure who made them.

Where the value
might actually sit.

Start with the operational bottleneck or commercial decision, not a list of tools.

01 / Account management

Find accounts that need attention

Combine performance changes, customer correspondence and commercial terms to surface retention risks, underdeveloped accounts and credible upsell opportunities. Let the account owner validate the recommendation.

Measure: faster intervention, qualified opportunities and retained or incremental revenue.

02 / Sales & partnerships

Spend less time preparing, more time selling

Build researched account briefs, qualify inbound leads, identify missing CRM information and suggest next steps. Keep commercial claims, outreach and relationship decisions in human hands.

Measure: preparation time, qualification accuracy and pipeline conversion.

03 / Game performance

Understand what's driving results

Bring studio, operator and promotional information into one view. Identify outliers and test whether a campaign changed portfolio profitability rather than simply shifting activity between games.

Measure: contribution margin, incremental revenue and post-promotion performance.

04 / Commercial operations

Reduce the work between the systems

Check onboarding information, route approvals, compare contract obligations with operating data and produce exception reports. The objective is fewer missed actions, not another dashboard no one opens.

Measure: cycle time, error rates and actions completed.

The difficult part

It rarely fails because the demo looked bad.

In iGaming, one account may touch an operator, a platform, an aggregator, a game studio and several reporting systems. Data definitions, permissions, commercial terms and incentives don't necessarily align.

A model can find patterns in the information it receives. It cannot independently repair missing attribution, reconcile incompatible revenue definitions or determine whether a commercial decision is appropriate.

And when personal data, customer vulnerability or regulated decisions are involved, governance and effective human oversight are part of the design, not something added before launch.

Why implementation is harder than it looks ↗

Start small.
Prove the value.

An AI pilot should be a business experiment, with measurable outcomes and an obvious stop button.

01 / Define

Choose one decision or workflow

Identify its owner, the current cost of getting it wrong or doing it slowly, and the result that would justify changing the process.

02 / Check

Test the inputs

Confirm data access, definitions, completeness, security and any approval or regulatory constraints before connecting the systems.

03 / Compare

Run a controlled pilot

Compare the assisted workflow with today's baseline. Track accuracy, saved effort, adoption, exceptions and the commercial result. Review errors, not just successes.

04 / Decide

Scale, change or stop

Proceed only where the improvement survives scrutiny. A pilot that shows why a proposed tool isn't worth implementing can be a very good result.
