Commercial leadership / Interim and fractional

Experienced leadership.
Without the full-time hire.

When a founder needs a commercial counterpart, an executive role is vacant or growth has stalled, a new permanent hire isn't always the first answer.

Fractional and interim leadership puts someone in charge of the decisions, people and work that need moving.

When it makes sense.

Not another advisory retainer that produces recommendations nobody owns.

01 / Leadership gap

Keep the commercial engine running

An executive departure or delayed hire can leave decisions waiting. Interim leadership can set priorities, maintain partner relationships and give teams an accountable point of ownership.

02 / Founder support

Turn a direction into an operating plan

Founders often know where they want to go but cannot personally run every deal, hire and account review. We help translate objectives into commercial decisions, named owners and a useful reporting rhythm.

03 / Growth reset

Fix the underlying execution

More activity rarely fixes weak qualification, poor forecasting or unmanaged customer expansion. Start with where revenue comes from, where deals stall and whether commercial ownership is clear.

04 / Market entry

Build before scaling the team

New-market entry needs a clear customer proposition, realistic routes to market and priorities for local partnerships. A focused leadership engagement can test the model before the fixed cost base grows.

Scope and accountability

Agree outcomes, then work backwards.

An engagement may include commercial planning, team leadership, strategic-account involvement, senior negotiations, partner development, pipeline management and recruitment support.

The first step is to agree the problem, authority to make decisions, operating cadence and what success will mean. Engagements can be time-bound projects or regular fractional involvement. We don't assume one model fits every business.

What gets measured.

A leadership role should be judged on progress that matters to the business, not days booked or slides delivered.

Commercial clarity

Target customers, market priorities, qualification criteria and a pipeline with decisions attached.

Revenue quality

Realistic forecast, margin, retention, expansion opportunities and whether the activity supports the operating plan.

Team effectiveness

Clear ownership, coaching priorities, useful reviews and fewer unresolved handovers.

Transition readiness

Documentation, management processes and sensible handover to a permanent executive when appropriate.
