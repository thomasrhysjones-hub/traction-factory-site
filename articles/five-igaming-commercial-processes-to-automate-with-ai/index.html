Five iGaming Commercial Processes Worth Automating with AI

Five realistic AI and automation use cases for iGaming sales, account management, promotions, onboarding and revenue reporting, with metrics and guardrails.

Thomas Rhys Jones·20 November 2026
Share on LinkedIn ↗

Most iGaming businesses don’t need an AI strategy deck to find their first useful application.

They need to identify the recurring work that consumes commercial time, delays decisions or hides opportunities. Then decide which parts can be automated, which benefit from AI and which should remain with the people responsible for the outcome.

Here are five workflows worth examining. None is a guaranteed return on investment. Each can be tested against an existing baseline.

1. Account health and opportunity reviews

The problem: Supplier or operator account managers spend time moving between revenue reports, correspondence, contract information and meeting notes. Important changes are easy to miss.

The workflow: A scheduled review brings together permitted account-level data, flags material performance changes, highlights overdue actions and proposes questions for the account owner.

Keep the human: The system should not assert why an account has declined without evidence. The account manager verifies the cause, decides whether to contact the customer and owns the commercial response.

Measure: Time to identify an issue, share of alerts judged useful, completed follow-ups and incremental or retained revenue where attribution is credible.

2. Lead research and sales preparation

The problem: Business development people spend substantial time turning incomplete lead information into something useful for a conversation.

The workflow: Collect approved CRM data, public company information and submitted enquiries. Produce a concise briefing covering the prospect’s business, likely relevance, known relationships, open questions and suggested next steps.

A good system makes its sources visible and labels assumptions. It should not fabricate people, commercial arrangements or supposed buying intent.

Keep the human: Decide whether the lead is worth pursuing, check the information and personalise the approach. Relationship-building isn’t a task to remove from sales.

Measure: Preparation time, factual error rate, CRM completeness, lead-to-meeting conversion and qualified opportunities.

3. Slot performance and promotion analysis

The problem: Reporting often celebrates lifts in rounds or GGR without distinguishing genuinely incremental performance from a temporary shift between titles.

The workflow: Combine studio and operator data where permissions allow, compare against agreed baselines, examine portfolio-level effects and identify what happened after the campaign.

AI can help surface anomalies and draft a clear analysis. The critical part remains the measurement design.

Keep the human: Review other promotions, placement changes, marketing activity and the costs incurred by each party before concluding whether the campaign created value.

Measure: Incremental net revenue or margin where measurable, portfolio-level impact, sustained performance and time spent preparing the review.

For a deeper discussion, see why slot promotions fail to drive real growth.

4. Partner onboarding and contract follow-through

The problem: Launches stall when commercial, finance, legal, compliance and integration teams are waiting on different information.

The workflow: Track required documents and milestones, flag missing approvals, summarise obligations from approved contracts and notify the person responsible for the next step.

Standard automation may handle most of the routing. Generative AI may help organise unstructured documents, but extracted terms need validation.

Keep the human: Approve commercial commitments, legal interpretations and regulated checks. A generated contract summary is not legal advice.

Measure: Onboarding cycle time, overdue dependencies, rework and time from agreement to commercial launch.

5. Revenue forecasting and exception reporting

The problem: A forecast can become an exercise in collecting spreadsheets and debating numbers, particularly when information arrives at different times or on different definitions.

The workflow: Use a consistent data model to assemble account forecasts, compare actual results with expectations and flag meaningful deviations. Add narrative explanations only where supporting evidence exists.

More sophisticated modelling isn’t always better. A transparent baseline can outperform a complex system built on weak information.

Keep the human: Check contract events, pipeline confidence, operator changes and one-off adjustments. Finance and commercial owners approve the forecast and its assumptions.

Measure: Forecast error, time spent reconciling reports, useful exceptions and the quality of decisions made from the output.

Which one should come first?

I’d prioritise workflows using four questions:

  1. Is the current problem expensive or frequent enough to matter? Saving five minutes a month is not a strategy.
  2. Are the inputs available and trustworthy? If not, fix the data issue first.
  3. Can a human check the output before it has consequences? Especially important when decisions affect people or regulated activities.
  4. Can we measure the change? Compare the new workflow against what happens today, including the time spent reviewing mistakes.

A practical first test might involve one team, one workflow and a clearly defined period. Compare quality, time, cost and actual outcomes before widening access.

Be cautious of tools that claim full autonomy without showing how they manage data errors, restricted information or uncertain conclusions.

AI should improve the work, not replace commercial accountability

The objective isn’t to automate the largest possible number of tasks.

It’s to reduce low-value effort and give people better information for the decisions that actually influence growth. In many cases, a mixture of straightforward automation, carefully scoped AI and experienced human judgement will outperform a grander implementation.
